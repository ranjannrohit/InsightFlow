Visualization Tutorial
Master chart selection and visual data communication for effective business decisions.
📊 Bar Chart Comparison

Compares quantities across discrete categorical groups using horizontal or vertical rectangles.

When to use: Comparing distinct categories or ranking values (e.g. Sales by Region, Revenue by Product).
Business Example: Identifying top-performing sales regions or comparing quarterly department expenses.
📈 Line Chart Trends Over Time

Displays continuous data trends and directional patterns over sequential time intervals.

When to use: Tracking continuous metric evolution (e.g. Monthly Revenue, Daily Website Traffic).
Business Example: Monitoring monthly active users (MAU) or tracking stock portfolio performance over 12 months.
📉 Area Chart Cumulative Volume

Similar to line charts, but fills the space below the line to emphasize total magnitude and volume.

When to use: Showing total volume trends over time (e.g. Cumulative MRR Growth, Total Bandwidth Consumption).
Business Example: Visualizing cumulative SaaS recurring revenue across customer tiers over quarters.
🥧 Pie Chart Proportions

Shows percentage breakdown of a whole using circular slices for small numbers of categories (<= 5).

When to use: Displaying high-level part-to-whole proportions with 2 to 5 distinct categories.
Business Example: Showing device breakdown (Desktop vs Mobile vs Tablet) for user traffic.
🍩 Donut Chart Share Breakdown

Circular ring chart displaying categorical proportions while reserving center space for a key total metric.

When to use: Categorical composition where the total sum metric is highlighted in the center.
Business Example: Displaying Total Revenue broken down by Customer Type (Enterprise vs SMB vs Consumer).
🌌 Scatter Plot Correlation

Plots points across two continuous numerical axes to reveal relationships, clusters, or outliers.

When to use: Analyzing correlations between two numeric variables (e.g. Marketing Spend vs Lead Volume).
Business Example: Identifying customer tenure vs annual spend or detecting outlier transactions.
🌡️ Heatmap Intensity Matrix

Uses color intensity in a 2D grid matrix to highlight concentration, hot spots, or activity spikes.

When to use: Spotting high-volume patterns across two dimensional grids (e.g. Day of Week vs Hour of Day).
Business Example: Analyzing customer support ticket spikes or server load density across peak hours.
🪵 Treemap Hierarchical Shares

Displays nested rectangular tiles sized according to value to show multi-level category proportions.

When to use: Comparing nested categories with large cardinality (e.g. Product Sub-categories within Departments).
Business Example: Visualizing total company budget allocation across main divisions and sub-teams.
🕸️ Radar Chart Multivariate Profile

Plots multiple quantitative metrics radiating from a central axis point to compare multi-attribute profiles.

When to use: Evaluating entity performance across 4 to 8 standardized dimensions (e.g. Skill Matrix, Product Ratings).
Business Example: Benchmarking vendor evaluations or assessing employee performance scorecards across key competencies.
